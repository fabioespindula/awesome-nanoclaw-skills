# Opportunity Patterns

Use this reference when `think-big` needs to produce opportunities, wedges, products, bets, experiments, or strategic options. Do not turn this into an execution roadmap unless the user asks.

## Opportunity Template

For each opportunity:

- **User/buyer**: who cares and who pays.
- **Change exploited**: what shift makes this newly possible or newly urgent.
- **Wedge**: narrow first use case.
- **Why now**: timing reason.
- **Mechanism**: why this could work.
- **Moat or durability**: data, workflow ownership, trust, distribution, compliance, brand, community, switching cost.
- **Risk**: why it may fail.
- **First experiment**: smallest useful test.

## Opportunity Patterns

### Workflow Compression

Turn a multi-step workflow into a single intent-driven flow.

Good for:

- professional services;
- internal operations;
- onboarding;
- compliance;
- support;
- sales admin.

Risk: users may still need control, auditability, or trust.

### Trust Layer

Create verification, reputation, audit, insurance, certification, permissions, or governance around a new capability.

Good when:

- capability outruns trust;
- buyers fear liability;
- agents act on behalf of users;
- regulators are circling.

Risk: can become slow services work unless productized.

### Vertical Depth

Apply a broad technology to a narrow domain with specific language, data, workflow, and compliance.

Good when:

- horizontal tools are too generic;
- domain context matters;
- buyers value outcomes over tools.

Risk: market may be too small or distribution too hard.

### Embedded Distribution

Win by appearing inside the system where the user already works, not as a separate destination.

Good when:

- users are saturated with apps;
- the workflow has a natural host platform;
- switching is hard but augmentation is acceptable.

Risk: platform dependency.

### New Buyer

The product succeeds because the buyer changes, not because the tool is better.

Examples:

- employee tool becomes manager tool;
- consumer behavior becomes employer benefit;
- developer tool becomes compliance requirement;
- founder tool becomes investor diligence layer.

Risk: selling motion may be harder than product.

### Anti-Trend Product

Build for the counter-reaction: privacy, human taste, slowness, local control, authenticity, durability, or craft.

Good when:

- dominant trend creates fatigue;
- users lose trust;
- institutions require human accountability.

Risk: can become aesthetic positioning without economic pull.

### Orchestration Layer

Coordinate many tools, agents, vendors, models, or workflows.

Good when:

- fragmentation is high;
- users need policy, routing, memory, billing, evaluation, or observability;
- many point solutions are emerging.

Risk: incumbents may absorb orchestration.

## Scoring Rubric

Use 1-5 unless the user asks for a different rubric.

### Relevance

1. Tangential to the prompt.
2. Related but generic.
3. Clearly relevant.
4. Highly relevant to the user's context.
5. Directly hits the core strategic question.

### Impact

1. Small workflow improvement.
2. Useful but local.
3. Meaningful for a niche or team.
4. Could change category economics or behavior.
5. Could reshape power, distribution, margins, or institutions.

### Feasibility

1. Mostly science fiction or blocked.
2. Possible but many dependencies.
3. Buildable with current tools but hard distribution/trust.
4. Buildable and testable now.
5. Already happening in pockets; needs focus or execution.

### Uncertainty

- **Low**: enough evidence and known constraints.
- **Medium**: plausible, but adoption or incentives unclear.
- **High**: speculative or dependent on multiple uncertain shifts.

## Experiment Types

Suggest experiments, not full plans, unless asked.

- landing page with real buyer interviews;
- concierge workflow;
- prototype with 5 expert users;
- manual service that simulates the future product;
- dataset or benchmark;
- paid pilot;
- shadow workflow inside an existing team;
- waitlist with qualification questions;
- fake-door feature in an existing product;
- expert review of regulatory/trust constraints.

## Opportunity Red Flags

- "Everyone will need this" with no specific buyer.
- Pure feature with no distribution path.
- No reason it becomes urgent now.
- Depends on perfect model behavior.
- Ignores trust, liability, compliance, or user control.
- Competes with a platform default without leverage.
- Big market, vague wedge.
